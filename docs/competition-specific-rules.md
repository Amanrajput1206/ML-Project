### Project Overview

This Kaggle competition is part of the **Machine Learning Course Project-I**. It evaluates your complete machine learning approach — data understanding, preprocessing, model experimentation, analysis of results, and final predictive performance. **The project will not be evaluated solely on the Kaggle leaderboard.**

- **Team Size:** 3–4 members
- **Submission Deadline:** 15th October 2026

### Model Restrictions

- Only models covered by the concepts taught in **Part 1 of the course** are permitted for this project.
- Experimenting with a variety of relevant models is required — a good score with a single model does not remove this requirement.

### Academic Integrity

- Participants must submit their own work and must not copy or reproduce another participant's code or solution.
- Sharing code, models, predictions, or other competition-specific solutions between teams is not allowed.
- Participants may discuss general concepts and approaches, but must not share implementation details that give another participant an unfair advantage.
- Participants must not attempt to access, obtain, or reverse-engineer the hidden test labels.
- Participants must not exploit bugs or vulnerabilities in the competition or evaluation system to gain an unfair advantage.
- Each participant must use only their own registered Kaggle account.
- Plagiarism, collusion, manipulation of the leaderboard, or any other form of unfair competition may result in disqualification.
- Participants must follow Kaggle's general competition rules and guidelines.

### Reproducibility

- Use **consistent random seeds/states** throughout your code.
- Ensure any randomness in your methods is controlled so results can be reliably reproduced.
- A **GitHub link** to your source code must be included in your report to ensure reproducibility of results.
